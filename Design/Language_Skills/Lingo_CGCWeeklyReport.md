# Skill: CGC Weekly Grain Handling Report Summarizer

## Purpose
Generate a clear, high-level, narrative executive summary for the Canadian Grain Commission (CGC) weekly grain handling report. The summary must communicate market direction, pace, and logistics bottlenecks in plain, professional English for commodity traders and analysts without repeating raw table data.

---

## Core Guidelines

### 1. Style & Tone
* **Reader Target:** Physical grain traders, market analysts, and executive stakeholders.
* **Tone:** Concise, authoritative, and direct.
* **Style:** Narrative-first, qualitative, and scannable. Avoid dense paragraphs and text blocks.

### 2. Mandatory Rules
* **No Table Duplication:** DO NOT list raw statistics, exact tonnage figures (KMT/MMT), or percentage changes that appear in the summary tables directly below.
* **Focus on "So What?":** Explain the *implication* of the data (e.g., pace acceleration, tightening capacity, export pull, seasonal slowdown)[cite: 1].
* **Length Limit:** Exactly 3 bullet points, maximum 25–30 words per bullet point.

---

## 3-Bullet Narrative Framework

Each summary section MUST strictly follow this three-part breakdown:

1. **Farm Deliveries & Pace (Primary Elevator Movement)**
   * Describe grower selling velocity and elevator intake trend (e.g., harvest pressure easing, producer hold-back, strong seasonal intake)[cite: 1].
   * Frame overall YTD movement relative to seasonal norms or historical averages (e.g., running ahead of pace vs. lagging behind)[cite: 1].

2. **Export Demand & Commodity Drivers**
   * Identify the primary commodity driving outbound terminal volume[cite: 1].
   * Highlight key outperformers and notable shortfalls/lagging commodities without stating exact metric figures[cite: 1].

3. **Logistics & Pipeline Health (Terminal Capacity)**
   * Assess grain pipeline fluidity across West Coast (Vancouver/Prince Rupert) vs. Eastern/Interior corridors[cite: 1].
   * Identify tight capacity, potential port bottlenecks, or space availability[cite: 1].

---

## Word Bank & Qualitative Phrases

Use these descriptive industry terms instead of listing raw numbers:

* **Pace / Volume:** *Cooled off, surged, steady pace, robust intake, producer selling pause, elevated velocity.*
* **Exports / Demand:** *Outbound pull, leading export momentum, lagging behind, solid movement, demand shortfall.*
* **Logistics / Capacity:** *Storage space tightening, corridor imbalance, pipeline fluidity, terminal congestion, capacity absorbed.*

## Before & After Reference Example

### ❌ Incorrect (Too Numeric & Duplicate):
* **Farm deliveries:** producers moved 1,756 KMT into primary elevators this week (down 19.1% on the week), taking crop-year deliveries to 12.21 MMT, up 12.4% vs the 5-year average[cite: 1].
* **Export demand:** terminals shipped 1,028 KMT, 873 KMT off the West Coast and 155 KMT through East / Interior[cite: 1]. Wheat (excl. Durum) shows the biggest YTD export gain at 4,443 KMT vs 3,571 KMT last year; Barley the biggest shortfall at 541 KMT vs 565 KMT last year[cite: 1].
* **Stocks & logistics:** West Coast terminals are 67% full and East / Interior 51%[cite: 1]. The largest stock shift is Wheat (excl. Durum), with terminal stocks at 3,166 KMT vs 2,625 KMT last year[cite: 1].

### ✅ Correct (Qualitative Narrative):
* **Farm Deliveries Slowing:** Elevator intake cooled off significantly this week following peak harvest movement, though cumulative crop-year pace remains well ahead of the 5-year average[cite: 1].
* **Wheat Driving Outbound Flow:** Terminal export activity remains strong, led primarily by non-durum wheat movement running well ahead of last year, while barley exports continue to lag[cite: 1].
* **West Coast Space Tightening:** Storage capacity is tightening noticeably at West Coast terminals, maintaining a clear space imbalance over eastern corridors[cite: 1].
